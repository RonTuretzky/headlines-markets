How trustless can
DKIM key provenance get?

The zk proof makes the email trustless given the key. The key always needs an oracle. This deck is about how small, ephemeral, and accountable that oracle can be made — grounded in DNS measurements, not vibes.

measured live 2026-08-20 · full memo: docs/DKIM-PROVENANCE.md · backlog A2
the problem

There is no key chain. There never was.

P(D, s, K, [t₀, t₁]) “the global DNS served key K at s._domainkey.D throughout [t₀, t₁]” — the one statement a registry must vouch for.
  • RFC 6376 defines no chaining. Each key is an independent TXT record; when NYT rotated to scph20250409, nothing was signed, endorsed, or referenced by its predecessor.
  • Emails can’t bootstrap it. A signed email proves possession of a private key — not that DNS ever published its public half.
  • The only trust root is control of a DNS zone at the moment you look. And nytimes.com’s zone isn’t DNSSEC-signed.
bounds on every design

Two hard truths

truth 1

DNS has no canonical state

There is no root commitment to “what DNS says” (except under DNSSEC). Split-horizon, GeoDNS, anycast, propagation: two honest observers can see different answers at the same instant. Without the zone owner’s signature, truth is only definable observationally — what k of n independent vantages saw over a window.

truth 2

The ceiling is DNS itself

Whoever controls the zone is the domain, per DKIM’s own model — every mail receiver on earth accepts their mail too. An oracle can at best faithfully report DNS; it cannot exceed it.

So the achievable goal, precisely: close the gap between what DNS actually served and what the registry believes — and make the residual trust small, ephemeral, and accountable. Never zero.

the key move · borrowed from certificate transparency

Trust can be made ephemeral
even when it can’t be eliminated

CT didn’t remove trust in CAs — it made CA misbehavior detectable and permanent. Transplant that: commit every key observation to an immutable log at observation time. Observers are trusted only at that instant; afterwards the record is math. A committee corrupted in 2027 cannot rewrite what it attested in 2026.

Corollary — you cannot observe the past. History that wasn’t witnessed is unrecoverable trustlessly. Every day the log isn’t running is provenance permanently lost. That’s the NYT archival problem inverted into an action item: start the log now.

the trilemma

Pick two

  • DNSSEC → ① + ③ going forward, but only for the few senders that sign their zones.
  • Observation quorums → ② + ③ going forward, with the trusted parties minimized, diversified, and bonded.
  • Archives (zk.email today) → ② + ③, but with full standing trust in one database.
  • ① + ② — zero trusted parties for arbitrary unsigned senders — does not exist. Anyone selling it is hiding an oracle.

the design space

Six tiers, ranked by trust eliminated

T0
DNSSEC — the only true proof
Zone owner signs the key record, chained to the ICANN root.
authority-rooted
T1
Notarized multi-vantage DNS quorum
zkTLS over DoH × resolvers × notaries × vantages × days. The workhorse for NYT.
observation-rooted
T2
Witnessed history log
Merkle log + daily onchain roots + cross-checking crawlers. Makes trust ephemeral.
time-rooted
T3
ARC verifier-concentration
Gmail’s seal collapses thousands of sender keys into one ultra-watched key.
verifier-rooted
T4
Policy hardening
Key aging, snapshot-at-open, selector scoping, in-circuit t= window binding.
policy
T5
Evidence-carrying optimistic registration

These compose — a real registry stacks T1+T2+T4+T5 for unsigned senders and drops to T0 wherever DNSSEC exists.

tiers in depth · 1 of 3

T0 authority  ·  T1 observation

T0 · DNSSEC
  • Verifiable onchain today via the ENS DNSSEC oracle pattern (~10⁶ gas, paid once per rotation) or zk-wrapped for cheaper settlement.
  • Available now for .gov, .bank, Proton — a genuinely trustless end-to-end exists, just not for NYT.
  • Leverage point: one ESP signing its zone flips every CNAME’d customer to T0 at once.
T1 · notarized DNS quorum — the workhorse
  • zkTLS over DoH: prove a TLS session with a cert-holding resolver (Cloudflare / Google / Quad9) returned TXT=K at time t.
  • Trust decomposes: resolver honesty × notary non-collusion × WebPKI (itself CT-logged) — multiplied across resolvers, notaries, vantages, and days.
  • Opacity’s MPC-TLS AVS is exactly this primitive — with EigenLayer slashing rails already built. Onchain cost = operator signature checks, far cheaper than DNSSEC chain verification.
  • Upgrades zk.email’s single unauditable updater key into a diverse, bonded, cross-checking observer set.
tiers in depth · 2 of 3

T2 witnessed history  ·  T3 ARC concentration

T2 · the log that makes trust ephemeral
  • Every observation (domain, selector, keyHash, resolver, vantage, t) appends to a Merkle log; root posted onchain daily — one storage write.
  • Independent crawlers cross-check (ours vs zk.email’s); divergence is an alarm.
  • Yields non-equivocation, permanence (upstream deletion no longer destroys provability), and immunity of committed history to later compromise.
  • Converts the archive from “trust our database” to a tamper-evident public record — for the cost of a cron job.
T3 · one giant key instead of thousands
  • Gmail seals inbound mail (d=google.com, s=arc-20160816 — live, unchanged since 2016): a signature over the content AND Google’s own dkim=pass verdict rendered against DNS at receipt.
  • Collapses per-sender provenance into provenance of one ultra-watched, slow-moving key.
  • Different trust shape, not strictly less: full trust in Google’s verdict, and google.com is itself unsigned — the root is still T1, but of a single key.
  • Best role: redundant second path that high-value settlements must agree with.
tiers in depth · 3 of 3

T4 policy  ·  T5 accountability

T4 · “DNS-true but malicious”

Perfect DNS fidelity doesn’t stop dangling-CNAME selector takeovers, brief insertions during a zone compromise, or stale parallel keys (NYT’s k2).

  • Key aging — settlement-eligible only after N days of continuous quorum observation.
  • Snapshot-at-market-open — pin the observed key set; mid-market keys enter via the aged path.
  • In-circuit t= ∈ observed window — small extension to the compiled E1 circuit.
  • “Old key signs new key” is impossible — aging + window-binding + witnessed history is the honest synthetic substitute.
T5 · evidence-carrying optimistic registration
  • Register with (keyHash, evidenceHash, bond) — verify nothing onchain on the happy path.
  • A challenge forces full verification of the pre-committed T1 evidence (or a DNSSEC counter-proof); the loser is slashed.
  • Optimistic about verification cost, never about evidence collection — DNS at challenge time may have legitimately moved on.
  • The dispute ladder must bottom out at T0/T1; watchers are natural — anyone holding the other side of the market.

the honest one-liner

Key provenance can’t be trustless in the absolute — DNS has no canonical state, and NYT doesn’t sign its zone. What we can do: shrink the trusted step to “k independent, bonded observers agreed on what DNS served, at one instant,” commit that instant immutably, and let everything afterward be mathematics.

Ephemeral. trusted only at the instant of observation
Diverse. resolvers × notaries × vantages — not one updater key
Slashable. bonded evidence, challengeable, punishable

…instead of standing, singular, and silent.
